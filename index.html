Active and Passive Voice

English Second Language · Grades 8–12 · Topic: Language Structures and Conventions

Start learning

1. Introduction and Learning Objectives

Subject English Second Language
Grade 8–12 (Namibian Junior and Senior Secondary Curriculum, NIED). Core lesson pitched at Grade 10; extension tasks for Grades 11–12.
Topic Language Structures and Conventions – Active and Passive Voice
Duration 2 lessons × 40 minutes

By the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:

  1. Identify whether a sentence is in the active or passive voice.
  2. Name the subject, verb and object in an active sentence.
  3. Change at least four active sentences into the passive voice in the correct tense.
  4. Explain when the passive voice is useful (e.g. news reports, science writing).

2. Learning Content

What is "voice"?

Voice tells us whether the subject of a sentence does the action or receives the action.

🟢 Active voice

The subject does the action.
The learner wrote the essay.

🔵 Passive voice

The subject receives the action.
The essay was written by the learner.

How to change active into passive

  1. Find the object of the active sentence and make it the new subject.
  2. Use the correct form of "to be" in the same tense as the original verb.
  3. Add the past participle of the main verb (written, eaten, built).
  4. Add "by" + the doer (optional if the doer is unknown or unimportant).
Object + form of "to be"same tense + past participle + (by + doer)

When do we use the passive voice?

  • When the doer is unknown: My phone was stolen.
  • When the doer is obvious or unimportant: The road was repaired last week.
  • In news reports and science writing: Rain was recorded in Windhoek on Monday.

Tip: Only verbs with an object (transitive verbs) can be passive. "She sleeps" cannot become passive.

3. ICT-Integrated Lesson Plan

Phase Teacher activity Learner activity ICT use
Introduction (5 min) Shows two newspaper headlines: "Police arrest thief" and "Thief arrested". Asks what is different. Discuss in pairs who does the action. Projected website.
Development (20 min) Explains the rule using the diagram, tense chart and video. Watch with captions, copy the chart, ask questions. Video, chart, diagram, text-size tools.
Activity (10 min) Moves between groups and guides. Sort sentences and rewrite sentences in the passive. Interactive activity with instant feedback.
Assessment (lesson 2) Monitors, reviews results. Complete the online quiz individually. Self-marking quiz.
Conclusion (5 min) Summarises; links to report writing. Write one passive sentence about their school (exit ticket). Downloadable notes.

Differentiation across grades: Grades 8–9 focus on present and past simple. Grades 10–12 add continuous, perfect and modal tenses and analyse passive voice in news articles.

How the website supports my teaching approach

I use a learner-centred, communicative approach. Learners discover the rule from real examples (headlines), practise it actively and receive instant feedback. The website lets learners revisit the content at their own pace, while the interactive tasks and quiz quickly show me which learners need extra help.

6. Support for Different Learning Needs

🔠 Adjustable text size and high contrast

Buttons at the top (A−, A+, ◐) enlarge text and switch to high-contrast colours. Supports: learners with low vision or dyslexia.

🎬 Captions and alternative text

The video has captions and the diagram has a written description for screen readers. Supports: learners with hearing or visual impairments.

📝 Simplified instructions

The rule in three easy steps. Supports: struggling readers and Grade 8 learners.

  • Move the object to the front.
  • Add "is / was / will be".
  • Add the verb in its 3rd form (eaten, written) + "by ...".

⬇️ Downloadable notes

Learners without internet at home can download and print notes. Supports: learners with limited connectivity.

7. Digital Citizenship

  • Be respectful online: Use kind language in comments and group chats; never bully or share hurtful content.
  • Respect copyright: Say where you got information, pictures or videos. Do not copy someone's writing and call it your own (plagiarism).
  • Protect your privacy: Never post your full name, address, phone number or photos online. This website does not collect or publish any learner information or photographs.
  • Use AI tools honestly: If you use a grammar checker or AI, say so and check the answer yourself.
